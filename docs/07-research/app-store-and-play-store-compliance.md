# App Store & Play Store Compliance

Researched 2026-08-18 against Apple's current App Review Guidelines and Google Play's current Developer Program Policy, cross-checked against this app's actual current state. **Updated 2026-08-19** with fresh research backing [ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md) (§2, §4, §5) — Sign in with Apple confirmed as a real, current 4.8 trigger (LinkedIn explicitly named); Google Play confirmed to have no equivalent Sign-in-with-Google mandate; Apple's regional "reasonable methods" adult-confirmation rule and its 2026 rollout trajectory confirmed. (not the aspirational design in [Safety Features Catalog](../03-architecture/safety-features-catalog.md)/[Safety UX Flows](../05-ux/safety-ux-flows.md) — what's genuinely built vs. what's still planned). This app hasn't been submitted to either store yet (still pre-launch per [ADR-005](../04-decisions/adr-005-invite-only-company-based-closed-launch.md)'s closed/invite-only plan), so nothing below is "we're in violation" — it's "fix before submission." Sources cited inline; this is web research, not a lawyer's opinion — see the disclaimer at the bottom.

**Why this matters more than average for this app**: both stores apply extra scrutiny to apps that connect strangers for real-world contact, and both have been actively tightening exactly these policies through 2026 (Apple's UGC guideline expansion in February, Google's dating-app age-verification rules in January, its account-deletion policy in April). This app sits squarely in the category both platforms are watching most closely right now.

## 1. Account deletion — required by both stores, not yet built

**Apple (Guideline 5.1.1(v), enforced since 2022)**: any app that allows account creation must let the user initiate account deletion from inside the app — not just deactivate/disable it, actually delete the account and its personal data. If Sign in with Apple is used, the app must also call Apple's REST API to revoke the token on deletion.

**Google Play (new, effective 2026-04-15, ~30-day compliance window from that date)**: same core requirement — account creation implies account deletion must be offered **both in-app and via a web resource**, with real data deletion, not deactivation. A "Data deletion" section is now part of the mandatory Data Safety form in Play Console.

**Current app status**: not built. `ProfilePage`'s "SIGN OUT" (`AuthSessionNotifier.signOut()`) ends the local session and revokes the refresh token — it does not delete the account or any data. There is no account-deletion flow anywhere in `frontend/` or `backend/`, and no web-based deletion path exists either. **This is the single most concrete, universally-required, currently-missing item** — both platforms will reject a submission without it, not just flag it.

## 2. Sign in with Apple — resolved by design, not yet built ([ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md), 2026-08-19)

**Guideline 4.8**: an app that offers any third-party/social login must also offer an equivalent alternative that meets specific privacy properties (limits data collection to name + email, lets the user hide their real email, doesn't use interactions for ad-tracking without consent) — Sign in with Apple automatically satisfies this and is the default way developers comply. Confirmed via fresh research: LinkedIn is one of the services Apple explicitly names as triggering this requirement, and the "exclusively uses your own account system" exception doesn't apply here — LinkedIn OAuth *is* the account system today ([ADR-006](../04-decisions/adr-006-progressive-linkedin-first-trust-onboarding.md), [ADR-011](../04-decisions/adr-011-linkedin-onboarding-slice-design.md)).

**Resolved by [ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md) (final shape 2026-08-19)**: exactly the design question this section previously raised ("does a Sign in with Apple user start at Level 0... and need to separately connect LinkedIn to reach Level 1a?") is now answered — yes. Account creation has four parallel paths: Sign in with Apple, Sign in with Google, email+password, and LinkedIn direct — the first three land at a real, persisted Level 0 (read-only); LinkedIn is the only path to Level 1. **Status: designed, not yet implemented** — see `backend/level0-federated-identity-PLAN.md` / `frontend/level0-federated-identity-PLAN.md`. Google Play has no equivalent "must offer Sign in with Google" mandate (confirmed via research) — adding it is a UX/parity choice, not a compliance requirement. The new email+password path doesn't interact with 4.8 at all — it's "your own account system," not a third-party login, so it neither triggers nor helps satisfy the guideline; Apple still just needs to stay equally prominent next to whichever third-party logins (Google, LinkedIn) are shown on iOS.

## 3. User-generated content: reporting and blocking — specified, not built

**Apple (Guideline 1.2, expanded February 2026)**: apps with UGC or social-networking features must let users report objectionable content in **two taps or fewer**, and must have a working block mechanism reviewers will actually test. The expanded guideline explicitly calls out apps with "random or anonymous chat" as squarely in scope — relevant once this app's messaging/chat feature (not yet built) ships, even though this isn't literally anonymous chat.

**Google Play**: similar UGC moderation expectations, plus "Other user-generated content" and "Other actions" are now explicit declarable data types in the Data Safety form.

**Current app status**: [Safety Features Catalog](../03-architecture/safety-features-catalog.md) specifies exactly this ("reporting should take seconds," a full report-reason taxonomy, immediate block/restrict/hide-profile/cancel-meetup on report) — but it's design, not code. The only report/block UI that exists today is `matches_page.dart`'s Report/Block buttons, which show a toast (`showSnack`) and do nothing else — no backend endpoint, no real moderation action. [Roadmap](../06-roadmap/roadmap.md) already lists "Reporting/blocking, basic trust score, new-account restrictions" as a Phase 1, not-yet-started item — this research doesn't change that priority, it raises the stakes on it: it's not just a safety feature anymore, it's a submission blocker for both stores once matching/chat go live.

## 4. Dating as a selectable intent — a real compliance risk, not just a scope-discipline one

**Google Play (new, since 2026-01-28)**: dating apps must enable "Restrict Declared Minors" in Play Console. Apps where dating/matchmaking is only an *incidental* feature are exempt from that specific setting, but only if they implement and maintain their own effective age-verification mechanism to keep minors out of the dating feature specifically. Google's Child Safety Standards policy also now requires social/dating apps to publish child-safety standards and name a designated child-safety contact.

**Current app status**: this directly compounds an issue `CLAUDE.md` already flags under "Scope gap vs [ADR-004](../04-decisions/adr-004-defer-dating-and-open-ride-sharing.md)" — `dating` is a full, selectable `IntentType` enum member appearing in `intent_picker_sheet.dart`'s list and `orbiting_intents.dart` on the landing page, even though [ADR-004](../04-decisions/adr-004-defer-dating-and-open-ride-sharing.md) defers real dating functionality to Phase 2/3 and it's currently modeled-but-inert. Previously this was flagged as a scope-discipline concern (don't build matching/chat/safety infra behind an intent that shouldn't be live yet). **It's now also a Play Store policy question**: an inert-but-selectable "Dating" option is still a dating/matchmaking feature from a policy-classification standpoint. **Partially addressed by [ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md) (2026-08-19)**: the new mandatory 18+ self-attestation at signup means the app is no longer completely age-gate-free — but self-attestation is explicitly *not* the "effective age-verification mechanism" Google's incidental-dating exemption calls for (that language implies something closer to verification, not a checkbox). So the underlying decision from this section is unchanged and still open: hide "Dating" from all UI until Phase 2 actually builds it properly (cheapest, matches [ADR-004](../04-decisions/adr-004-defer-dating-and-open-ride-sharing.md)'s original intent), or invest in real age verification if it stays visible. Given Phase 1 doesn't need dating live at all, hiding it is still very likely the right call — flagging as a decision for Shashika, not deciding it here.

## 5. Age rating

Apple's rating bands were overhauled in 2025: 4+ / 9+ / 13+ / 16+ / 18+ (replacing the old 4+/9+/12+/17+ scheme). Apps with user profiles or messaging need at least 16+/18+; given this app facilitates real-world meetups with strangers (and has a dormant `dating` intent, however it's ultimately resolved), **18+ is the defensible choice, not 16+** — worth deciding deliberately in App Store Connect rather than defaulting low. **New as of [ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md) (2026-08-19)**: starting 2026-02-24, Australia/Brazil/Singapore require 18+-rated apps to confirm adulthood via "reasonable methods," not self-attestation alone, and both stores have signaled this expanding through 2026. This app's new age-eligibility checkbox ([ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md)) satisfies the *base* "ask" requirement everywhere else, but won't satisfy that stricter bar if/when Sri Lanka or other launch markets are added to it — tracked as a known, not-yet-solved gap, not something to treat as closed.

## 6. Background location — strict, specific disclosure requirements if the Safety Gate's live-location feature works while backgrounded

Google Play requires a **prominent, in-app** disclosure (not buried in a privacy policy or settings menu) before requesting background location — it must explicitly use language like "background" or "when the app is closed," describe what's collected and why, and be shown during normal app use, not on request only. Play Console also requires a separate permissions-declaration form plus a demo video for background location access specifically.

**Current app status**: [Safety UX Flows](../05-ux/safety-ux-flows.md)'s live-location-sharing-during-a-meetup feature isn't built yet (queued behind the meetup-scheduling slice currently in progress), but its intended use case — sharing location with a trusted contact while walking to/attending a meetup — strongly implies it needs to keep working if the app is backgrounded, not just foregrounded. Worth deciding *now*, before that feature is built, whether it truly needs background location or can be scoped to foreground-only for v1 (much simpler policy story) — this is exactly the kind of thing cheaper to decide before building than to retrofit after.

## 7. Privacy policy and Data Safety / App Privacy declarations

Both stores require a public, hosted privacy policy URL and structured in-console data disclosures (Apple's "App Privacy" nutrition label, Google's Data Safety form) describing every category of data collected (phone number, personal/work email, legal name, address, LinkedIn profile data, precise location, verification codes) and every third party it's shared with or processed by (Twilio, Resend, LinkedIn, the eventual map/geocoding vendor, Firebase/FCM once wired). **No privacy policy currently exists anywhere in this vault or repo** — [Sri Lanka PDPA - Work Email Verification](sri-lanka-pdpa-work-email-verification.md) covers the Sri Lankan legal-compliance angle for one specific data type, but that's a legal-review research note, not a published, user-facing privacy policy. This needs to exist, accurately, before either store submission — and accuracy matters here specifically because both stores actively audit apps for privacy-label/policy mismatches with actual data flows.

## Priority summary

Roughly in "would block submission outright" order:

1. Account deletion (in-app + web for Android) — nothing exists yet, required by both stores unconditionally.
2. A real, published privacy policy + accurate data-safety/privacy-label declarations — required by both, nothing exists yet.
3. Sign in with Apple — **designed ([ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md), 2026-08-19), not yet built.** Was the top open gap; now has a concrete plan (`backend/level0-federated-identity-PLAN.md`, `frontend/level0-federated-identity-PLAN.md`).
4. Reporting/blocking with actual backend effect — already on [Roadmap](../06-roadmap/roadmap.md) Phase 1, this research raises its priority once matching/chat ship, since it stops being "just" a safety feature and becomes a submission requirement.
5. Resolve the dating-intent visibility question — still open. [ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md)'s age checkbox helps but doesn't resolve it (see §4) — still a choice between hiding "Dating" or building real age verification.
6. Age rating — target 18+ deliberately (not 16+) given real-world stranger meetups; [ADR-014](../04-decisions/adr-014-federated-base-identity-optional-linkedin-age-eligibility.md)'s self-attestation checkbox covers the baseline "ask" requirement but not the stricter "reasonable methods" bar some regions now require (see §5).
7. Background location scope decision — cheaper to decide "foreground-only for v1" now than to retrofit the stricter background disclosure flow later, if backgrounded sharing isn't actually essential for launch.

## Disclaimer

This is web research summarized by Claude, not legal advice, and app store policies change often — both platforms updated multiple relevant policies within 2026 alone, several after this doc's research date could exist by the time this is read. Confirm current requirements directly against Apple's and Google's own policy pages before submission, and get an actual legal review for the account-deletion data-handling requirements and the Sri Lanka PDPA question already flagged in [Action Tracker](../00-project/action-tracker.md) — those two intersect (deleting an account fully vs. retaining a ban-evasion fingerprint, per [Action Tracker](../00-project/action-tracker.md)'s already-flagged retention tension).
